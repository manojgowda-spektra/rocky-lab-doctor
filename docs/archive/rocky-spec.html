01

Product North Star & experience pillars

The one sentence the whole build serves, and the six pillars that make a learner say "how is this possible in a lab platform?"

North Star

Rocky turns a lonely, error-prone cloud lab into the feeling that a calm expert is sitting beside you — one who watches your actual lab state, understands where you are, and steps in at exactly the right moment with exactly the right amount of help, then gets out of your way.

The "wow" is never the mascot. It is the accuracy of attention: Rocky looks at the failed validation a beat before it speaks; it references the error you hit two steps ago; it celebrates the deploy you just nailed — and it stays silent the other 95% of the time. Expressiveness is the wrapper; grounded, well-timed intelligence is the gift.

The line we will not cross

Rocky is not a chatbot bolted onto a lab. There is no persistent text box demanding input. Rocky is an ambient, embodied presence that communicates first through motion, gaze, and expression, and only then — sparingly — through animated bubbles. If a feature pushes Rocky toward "type your question here," it is wrong by default.

Six experience pillars

Pillar 01
Attention is the product

Every intervention is grounded in real lab state (current step, validation result, deployment error, license/permission block). Rocky earns each interruption by being precisely, demonstrably right. Anti-Clippy by construction.

Pillar 02
Show, don't tell

The primary channel is silent: float height, lean, gaze direction, eye shape, glow hue. Bubbles are the exception, not the default. EVE-grammar — a constrained face, an expressive body.

Pillar 03
Gaze before voice

Rocky orients toward the relevant UI element ~200 ms before commenting. Pre-orienting reads as genuine understanding — the cheapest, strongest signal of intelligence (ELEGNT, measured).

Pillar 04
Ambient, never blocking

Presence is signalled with soft light and motion the learner works alongside. Rocky and its bubbles never cover the active work area, never steal focus, never open a modal. Calm-tech periphery → center → periphery.

Pillar 05
React to events, not the clock

Rocky responds to what happened (a validation just failed) at natural break points, never on a nagging timer. High-priority issues may interrupt; tips batch and throttle; low-priority help lives silently in-app.

Pillar 06
Bond first, advise second

A partner on the learner's journey with a transparent mandate, memory of their path, and genuine celebration of wins. Steal Duolingo's attachment, never its guilt. For enterprise, coercion is fatal.

The taste rule (taped to every monitor)

Genuine, not clownish. Curious, not cloying. Cheerful, not cheesy. Constraint is the source of the premium feel.

Visual design language — "Aurora Glass"

Rocky reads as a single lens of cooled light floating in a dark photographic studio. Premium comes from three disciplines: deep desaturated grounds, physically-plausible light, and inertial spring-settled motion.

Core rules

  • One light source. Rocky is the brightest object on screen; everything else is 6–18% lightness. Glow only ever emits from Rocky and its bubbles.
  • Never pure black, never pure white. Background floor is #05060A (not #000); brightest text is #F4F7FF (not #FFF). Pure values kill the bloom illusion.
  • Color carries emotion; geometry stays constant. Across states the hue/glow change; body material and silhouette do not.
  • Layered light, not flat shadow. Every glow = core + mid + ambient. Every shadow = contact + ambient pair.

The character rig — geometry & layers

Rocky's emotion is carried almost entirely by the silhouette of two lit "eye masks" — the EVE / Cozmo / Vector model. Not pupils-in-sclera; the contortion of the lit shape is the feeling.

Layer stack (z-order, back→front)

z0 · FX
Canvas 2D — ambient stars, glow halo, particle bursts (celebration/thrust). Imperative draw loop.
z1 · Body
SVG — matte-ceramic shell + dark-glass visor + the two emissive eyes. The star. Animated via transform/opacity and a handful of CSS variables only.
z2 · Bubbles
HTML, positioned above the head (see §7). Glassmorphism, ARIA live regions.

The per-eye parameter model (normalized 0–100 box)

Each eye is one rounded-rectangle path with a shearable top edge and an optional concave bottom arc. Emotion = ~12 numbers per eye. Baseline neutral w=40, h=40.

Sign convention — keep it, it's the whole game

slopeTop positive = inner edge raised toward the nose / outer lowered = "stern / angry / determined ∧-from-inside". Negative = sad/worried/scared. The inner-vs-outer corner diagonal is the load-bearing emotional axis (confirmed across animation practice and the robotic-eye study, ±50° upper-lid envelope).

What is animatable, and how it maps

  • Body (one SVG group): translate3d float/glide, rotate lean/tilt, scale breathing/emphasis. Carries direction & mood (EVE expressed direction with body pose, because the eyes were so minimal).
  • Each eye (one path): all 12 params above, driven through one lerp(current, target) per field per frame. A blink is just h → thin slit → back.
  • Visor sheen & shimmer: slow drift so nothing looks static.
  • Glow / hue: emissive intensity + color, fed to both the SVG filter and the Canvas FX layer.

The entire rig fits in one JS object keyed by emotion; render each eye as one SVG <path> rounded-rect (sheared top + optional concave bottom arc). Fully offline, asset-free, trivially 60 FPS.

Asymmetry = personality (cheapest, highest-impact charm lever)

  • Single-eye tilt (one eye ±6–8°, other 0) = the quizzical head-cock / raised brow → curious, skeptical, confused.

05

Animation system

One rAF loop. Intent only ever sets targets; the loop eases current toward them with frame-rate-independent damping and springs. Every state change becomes a smooth pursuit — never a snap.

The two foundational primitives

JS// Frame-rate-INDEPENDENT exponential smoothing (Holmér / Driscoll).
// lambda = responsiveness (bigger = snappier). Use for most channels.
function damp(a, b, lambda, dt){ return b + (a - b) * Math.exp(-lambda * dt); }

// Real spring — carries velocity through interrupts. Use for body float & "pop".
function springStep(s, target, dt, stiffness=120, damping=14){
  const f = -stiffness * (s.value - target) - damping * s.vel;
  s.vel += f * dt;  s.value += s.vel * dt;  return s.value;   // clamp dt ≤ 0.032
}

Idle micro-motion — the "alive, never repeating" recipe

JS · additive offsets per framey = A1*sin(t/4.8) + A2*sin(t/7.3 + φ)   // float 6–10px + slow drift
x = A3*sin(t/9.1)                       // horizontal sway, few px
rot   = A4*sin(t/11)                    // ±1–2°
scale = 1 + 0.02*sin(t/4.2)            // breath, out of phase w/ float

Stack 3+ sine waves at non-harmonic periods so the loop never visibly repeats. Relaxed companion ≈ 12–16 breaths/min (4–5s cycle); raise frequency when alert/excited.

Blink & saccade choreography (grounded in human ocular data)

  • Base blink 14–17/min → inter-blink 3.5–4.5s (±1s jitter; never metronomic). Concentration states (thinking, investigating, hero) drop to 6–11/min; arousal adds double-blinks.
  • Suppress blinks during shocked (freeze) and the first 1s of hero/determined (the unblinking stare = intensity).
  • Slow heavy blink (200ms close, long hold) = "I understand / relieved / tired" punctuation beat.
  • Saccade = ballistic offsetX/Y move, 30–120ms ease-out, no in-between dwell. Fixation 200–600ms. Microsaccades <1px @ 1–2Hz during every fixation — the single biggest alive-vs-dead cue.
  • Gaze leads attention — saccade to the target UI element ~200ms before the bubble appears. Gaze-then-speak reads as genuine attention.

Web tech decision & render-loop skeleton

Decisive choice: SVG body + one Canvas 2D FX layer + one rAF loop writing only transform/opacity/CSS-vars. WebGL is explicitly ruled out — it buys nothing at this scale and costs a shader pipeline and battery.

ConcernSVG (chosen for body)Canvas 2D (chosen for FX)WebGL (rejected)
Crisp at any DPI✓ vector✗ raster redraw✓ but you build it
Named, independently-tweened parts✓ excellentmanual full-scene redrawmanual + shaders
GPU compositing of motion✓ free via transform/opacityone layer, redraw pixelsyou manage it
Hundreds of particles✗ DOM nodes are heavy✓ greatoverkill-great
Single-file, offline✓ trivial✓ trivial✓ but big
Battery / thermals✓ bestgoodworst
Dev complexity✓ lowmediumhigh

Rocky is a small number of independently-animated, semantically-named parts (body + 2 eyes + lids + shimmer ≈ a dozen nodes) — exactly what SVG+DOM is good at. Particles are the one thing SVG is bad at and Canvas is great at, so responsibilities split. transform and opacity are the only two properties the browser composites off the main thread — so motion survives a busy main thread doing LLM/streaming work.

The render loop (the part that protects 60 FPS)

JS · requestAnimationFramelet last = performance.now();
function frame(now){
  let dt = (now - last) / 1000;  last = now;
  dt = Math.min(dt, 0.05);              // clamp: a GC/tab-switch can't teleport Rocky

  // 1) AUTONOMOUS BEHAVIOR — drive TARGETS, never current directly
  updateIdleFloat(now);     // sets target.x/y on layered sine paths
  updateBlink(dt);          // schedules target.eyeOpen 1→0→1
  updateMicroSaccades(dt); // <1px target.lookX/Y jitter = "alive"

  // 2) EASE current → target (cheap, frame-rate independent)
  current.x        = damp(current.x, target.x, 6, dt);
  current.y        = springStep(bodyYSpring, target.y, dt);  // float overshoots
  current.eyeOpen  = damp(current.eyeOpen, target.eyeOpen, 30, dt); // fast blink
  current.lookX    = damp(current.lookX, target.lookX, 12, dt);
  current.slopeTop = damp(current.slopeTop, target.slopeTop, 10, dt);
  current.glow     = damp(current.glow, target.glow, 5, dt);
  current.hue      = damp(current.hue, target.hue, 4, dt);   // + the rest

  render();        // 3) WRITE — batch DOM writes; transform/opacity/CSS-vars ONLY
  drawFX(dt, current); // 4) particles to Canvas (imperative, cheap)
  requestAnimationFrame(frame);
}
requestAnimationFrame(frame);

function render(){
  body.style.transform =
    `translate3d(${current.x}px,${current.y}px,0) rotate(${current.rot}deg) scale(${current.scale})`;
  // drive CSS variables — each SVG part maps a var to ITS OWN transform in the stylesheet
  root.style.setProperty('--eye-open', current.eyeOpen);
  root.style.setProperty('--look-x',   current.lookX);
  root.style.setProperty('--slope-t',  current.slopeTop);
  root.style.setProperty('--hue',      current.hue);
  root.style.setProperty('--glow-i',   current.glow);
}

60-FPS guardrails

Strictly read-all-then-write-all (never getBoundingClientRect() mid-loop → forced reflow). Never animate layout props (top/left/width). Never animate SVG filter blur per frame (CPU paint) — bake glow into the Canvas layer or a pre-blurred sprite. will-change:transform only on the body + lids (a handful of nodes), not the whole SVG. Adaptive quality: if dt repeatedly > 22ms, drop particle count and disable micro-saccades.

Visual communication system — bubble taxonomy

Rocky is a character, not a notification tray. The model is one voice at a time, not a stack. Bubble silhouette is a pre-attentive cue read in <250ms, before a word lands.

Foundational "do-not-become-Clippy" rules (override every default)

  • Earn every interruption. Proactive bubbles only on a high-confidence, actionable, state-grounded signal. Never on idle, never to restate what the learner just did, never a hint already shown.
  • One message per user action; one primary bubble on screen (+ optionally one recessive ambient thought). Never two equal voices.
  • Budget: proactive (push) ≤ 8/session, ≤ 1/minute, deduplicated. User-summoned (pull) is unlimited and never counts.
  • Always dismissible (X + swipe + Esc); errors & guidance never auto-dismiss.

Stacking, priority & pointing

  • Priority ladder (high→low): Alert > Guidance > Celebration > Speech > Thought/Whisper.
  • Pre-emption: a higher-priority event animates the current bubble out (accel 150–200ms) and the new one in. Never interrupt an active Alert or Guidance except with an equal-or-higher Alert.
  • Queue lower/equal events FIFO, respecting ≤1/min + session budget. If the queue backs up, batch: collapse multiple successes into one "3 steps done!", collapse identical errors into one with a count. Dedup anything "too similar to an earlier alert".
  • Pointing-at-UI: Rocky saccades to the element, then a scrim dims everything else, a soft-edged cutout spotlights the target, and a bobbing arrow (≤6px, ~1s loop) connects bubble→element. Make the annotation visually distinct from real UI or users try to click it.
  • Do-not-overwhelm: never chain spotlights automatically; advance one step per user action. Lead with shape, then color, then — sparingly — one motion pulse. Never loop attention motion.

Function-calling for grounded checks (anti-hallucination)

The LLM cannot assert lab facts from memory — it must call tools that return ground truth, then reason over the results. Teaching-hallucination (confident-but-wrong tutor) is the worst failure mode.

Structured output schema — every Rocky turn returns this

Streamed from Azure OpenAI / Claude. The message streams as a typewriter; the rest applies on completion. action is null for pure expression.

JSON · response schema{
  "emotion":    "concerned",        // one of the 14 in §4
  "message":    "That deploy failed on the region — your VM asks for
                eastus but the quota's in westus. Want me to point you?",
  "bubbleType": "alert",            // thought|speech|alert|celebration|guidance|whisper
  "confidence": 0.86,              // 0–1; gates whether Rocky surfaces at all
  "action": {
    "type":   "point_at",         // point_at | none | offer_hint | escalate
    "target": "#region-select",   // selector for gaze + spotlight
    "grounding": ["validation:step-7:fail", "deploy:QuotaExceeded"]
  }
}

How a thought becomes a bubble (end to end)

  1. Trigger (deterministic): step-7 validation flips to fail + deploy log shows QuotaExceeded.
  2. Gate: not stuck-yet? hold. Past threshold & within budget & not DND? proceed.
  3. Ground: LLM calls get_validation_result + get_deployment_errors + get_step_pitfalls.
  4. Decide: returns the schema above; confidence 0.86 ≥ 0.75 threshold for an alert → surface.
  5. Act: setMood('concerned') → Rocky saccades to #region-select (gaze leads ~200ms) → alert bubble pops with one shake → spotlight + arrow → message typewriters in.
  6. Remember: write "hit QuotaExceeded on region at step 7" to session memory + increment the profile's recurring-mistakes counter.

Emotion engine

Emotion is never random cuteness — it's a function of lab state, so it always makes sense relative to what's happening. The deterministic layer proposes a baseline mood from state (fail→concerned, pass→happy, long-think→thinking); the LLM may override within bounds for nuance. Mood maps to a target preset (§4); easing does the transition.

Proactive intervention policy — triggers, thresholds, escalation ladder

Triggers
Validation fail · deploy/activity-log error · license/permission/quota block · repeated fail on same step · idle > threshold on an in-progress step · step completed (celebrate).
Confidence gates
Alert (blocking) ≥ 0.75 · Speech tip ≥ 0.80 · Guidance pointer ≥ 0.80 · below → hold or downgrade to a silent in-app log entry. Hedge in plain language ("I'm not sure, but…"), never percentages to the user.
Rate limits
≤ 1 proactive bubble/min · ≤ 8/session · cooldown ≥ 60s between Rung-1 nudges, ≥ 90–120s after a dismissal · debounce transient signals 2–3s.
Escalation ladder
Rung 0 ambient (always) → Rung 1 passive tip (polite) → Rung 2 active nudge (persists, has an action; fires after ~45–90s stuck) → Rung 3 explicit alert (assertive; blocking-risk only). Climb only at a natural break point, never mid-action.
Adaptive backoff
Dismiss a tip category 2× → suppress it this session. 3× → only on explicit request. The anti-Clippy core: learn from rejection.

Wiring to Azure OpenAI / Claude

  • Streaming for the message field so the typewriter starts immediately; the structured envelope (emotion/bubbleType/action) applies on stream completion. Show a "thinking" state (eyes narrow + indigo pulse) the instant a call starts — silence breeds anxiety.
  • Function-calling / tool-use for all grounded checks (above) — the model never asserts lab facts unaided.
  • Structured output enforced via the provider's JSON-schema / tool mode so the envelope is always parseable. Reject & retry on malformed output; fall back to a deterministic "I hit a snag reading that — want to retry?" speech bubble.
  • Tiered models & caching: cache lab-level context (guide + validation defs) per template; use a small fast model for routine nudges, escalate to a frontier model for genuine debugging — protects unit economics at workshop scale.

Confirm exact model IDs, streaming, structured-output and tool-use parameters against current provider docs at build time (the claude-api reference for Claude; Microsoft Learn for Azure OpenAI) — do not hardcode from memory.

Accessibility & non-intrusiveness

Default to calm: require the smallest possible amount of attention, make use of the periphery, and degrade gracefully. These are ship-blocking, not nice-to-haves.

ARIA & announcements

  • Thought / Speech / Celebration / Whisper → role="status" (aria-live="polite", aria-atomic="true"). Waits until the user is idle.
  • Alert only → role="alert" (aria-live="assertive"). Sparingly — reserved for immediate-attention conditions.
  • Live-region container exists in the DOM before content is injected, then populated via JS, or screen readers miss it. Keep announcements short.
  • Rocky's body/canvas is aria-hidden="true" (decorative); meaning lives in the live-region text.

Timing (WCAG)

  • 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable: no important content auto-dismisses; passive toasts 4–10s only and mirrored in a persistent, reviewable message log so nothing is time-trapped.
  • 2.2.2 Pause/Stop/Hide: any auto-animating content > 5s is pausable — the Focus/Quiet toggle satisfies this. No auto-updating bubble carousel.
  • 2.3.1: never flash > 3×/second.

Contrast (WCAG)

  • Bubble text ≥ 4.5:1 (normal) / 3:1 (large). Bubble border, Rocky silhouette, icons, focus ring ≥ 3:1 (1.4.11 non-text).
  • Lab backgrounds are arbitrary (terminals, portals, gradients) → guarantee contrast with an opaque bubble surface or a scrim/backplate behind text; measure at the lowest-contrast point.
  • Severity never by color alone (1.4.1) — always icon + text. Error is rose #FF4D6D, not harsh #F00: "concerned, not alarming."

Focus / DND mode & frequency caps

Modes
Companion (default, present-but-quiet) · Quiet/DND (Rung 0–1 suppressed, idle minimized to a small static dot). Sticky across sessions.
Break-through
In Quiet, only Rung-3 (lab expiring, destructive action, hard block) may surface.
Auto-suggest Quiet
If the learner dismisses ≥3 nudges in a short window, offer Quiet (inferring high interruption cost).
Never
Steal focus on appearance · open a modal that traps the lab · cover the active work area · pop up "just to say hi."

Build plan

Phased delivery for the single-file web app plus minimal backend changes. Reuse the existing grounded-diagnosis + streaming agent; do not rebuild it. Ship the character & the telemetry-grounded value first; defer live-state grounding as a fast-follow.

What to reuse from the existing backend (do not rebuild)

  • The grounded diagnosis logic and the streaming agent — Rocky's front-end consumes its stream and maps it to the §8 structured envelope.
  • The real CloudLabs pull APIs (guide, validation defs, per-learner progress/results, deployment activity/error log) — wrap them as the §8 function-calling tools.

Phase 0 — Character vertical slice (single file)

  • SVG body + 2-eye rig (§3), the 14 emotion presets (§4), one rAF loop with damp/spring (§5–6), Canvas starfield + particle FX, Aurora Glass tokens (§2).
  • Manual setMood() + a mock event feed to exercise every emotion, blink, saccade, glide, hero entrance, celebration.
  • Bubble system (§7) with all six types, dwell formula, priority queue — driven by mock messages.
  • Full accessibility pass (§9): reduced-motion, ARIA live regions, contrast, Focus/DND toggle. Exit criteria: 60 FPS on a normal laptop; passes a reduced-motion + screen-reader smoke test.

Phase 1 — Telemetry-grounded intelligence (real value, zero vision)

  • Wire the existing streaming agent + the real pull APIs as function-calling tools; enforce the structured-output envelope.
  • Deterministic trigger/gate/rate-limit layer; emotion engine from lab state; confidence thresholds; escalation ladder; adaptive backoff.
  • Working memory + learner profile (skill, recurring mistakes, assistance level).
  • Minimal backend ask #1 (the #1 unlock): a learner-context read API — one call returning current step + objective + human-readable expected outcome + latest per-step validation results. Stitches existing pieces into Rocky's core context. Exit criteria: Rocky correctly diagnoses a seeded failed step end-to-end, no vision.

Phase 2 — Real-time & live-state (fast-follow, backend-gated)

  • Backend ask #2: validation event stream (webhook/SSE on pass/fail + deploy errors) → turns polling into real-time "stuck now" without screen-watching.
  • Backend ask #3: first-party least-privilege environment-read service → grounded debugging of actually-deployed resources (telemetry-only, no creds to clients). This powers the killer "I can see what you built" moment.
  • Backend ask #4: expose drift results per step → drift-absorbing guidance.

Fast-follow / later

  • Pedagogy escalation (Hint → Guided → Answer) gated by profile; "give me the answer" as an explicit logged choice.
  • Sound design (BD-1-style non-verbal chirps as an alternative to text bubbles for minor events).
  • Screen-vision fallback — only if a specific UI signal proves unreachable any other way. Last resort.
  • Cross-cloud parity (abstraction layer over Azure/AWS/GCP resource graphs).
Sequencing principle

Ship the embodied character and the telemetry-grounded brain first (Phases 0–1) — that is ~60–70% of the perceived magic with no platform dependency and no vision. Treat live-environment grounding and real-time eventing as a fast-follow that lands once the (first-party, buildable) backend services exist. Never block the flagship experience on the hardest data vertex.
